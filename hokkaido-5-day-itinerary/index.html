01Day by day

Each day has two or three plans to choose from and an indoor plan for bad weather. These are the neighbourhoods and the main stops; the times and the order are in the download.

  1. D1

    Day 1: Sapporo's winter lights

    Odori Park

    The White Illumination along Odori Park, mulled wine at the Munich Christmas Market and soup curry for dinner. Or the TV Tower deck first, looking straight down the lit-up park, then walk it.

    Rain plan: Sapporo Clock Tower and Akarenga, the red-brick former government office

  2. D2

    Day 2: Beer history, or a mountain-top view

    Sapporo

    The Sapporo Beer Museum, its tasting hall and Genghis Khan grilled lamb at the beer garden next door. Or Maruyama Park and Hokkaido Shrine in the snowy woods, then the streetcar and ropeway up Mt Moiwa for the night view.

    Rain plan: Shiroi Koibito Park, the biscuit factory

  3. D3

    Day 3: Canal, glass and sushi street

    Otaru

    The warehouse-lined canal, the merchant houses of Sakaimachi Street, Kitaichi Glass and its oil-lamp café, lunch on sushi street, the Music Box Museum and LeTAO's cheesecake.

    Rain plan: The Bank of Japan Otaru Museum and the music boxes

  4. D4

    Day 4: One limited express each way

    Asahikawa or Noboribetsu

    Polar bears, penguins and seals at Asahiyama Zoo, then Asahikawa ramen. Or Noboribetsu: the steaming boardwalk of Jigokudani and a long soak at a day-bathing hall facing the valley.

    Rain plan: Upopoy, the National Ainu Museum, at Shiraoi

  5. D5

    Day 5: Morning market, then New Chitose

    Sapporo

    Crab, sea urchin and kelp at Nijo Market, then the Rapid Airport train.

    Rain plan: Straight to the airport for Royce' Chocolate World

02Before you go

Things that change how you plan the trip, from the research behind the itinerary.

  1. Pick your dates around the lightsThe White Illumination and the Munich Christmas Market both run from November 20 to December 25. Outside those dates, day 1 loses its main event.
  2. Reserve limited express seatsThe trains to Asahikawa and Noboribetsu are all reserved-seat. The plan's fares include the seat: ¥5,440 each way to Asahikawa, ¥4,890 to Noboribetsu.
  3. Carry cash for NoboribetsuThe Donan Bus from Noboribetsu Station up to the onsen town doesn't accept IC cards.
  4. Asahiyama Zoo closes early in winterFrom November 11 to December 29 it opens 10:30 to 15:30, with last entry at 15:00, so the plan takes an early train.
  5. Check midweek closings in Otaru and AsahikawaMasazushi and the Bank of Japan museum in Otaru close on Wednesdays, Hachiya ramen on Thursdays, and Upopoy on Mondays.
  6. Weather can stop the ropewayMt Moiwa's ropeway and Hokkaido's trains are at the mercy of snow. Every day has an indoor plan you can switch to on the page.

04Asahiyama, Noboribetsu or Upopoy?

Asahiyama Zoo is the one for animals in the snow: the Lilac or Kamui limited express to Asahikawa, then about 40 minutes by bus.

Noboribetsu is the one for a hot spring. Jigokudani is free and open all day; the day-bathing hall is ¥2,500.

Upopoy is the bad-weather choice, with the museum indoors and the cheapest fare of the three. It closes on Mondays.
