Narita to Tokyo on the Skyliner

NRT
Two charges, one rideChecked 2026-10-30

AI-assisted content: drafted with AI tools, then fact-checked and edited by Route Lab.

If you land at Narita and you're staying anywhere near Ueno, Asakusa or the east side of Tokyo, the Keisei Skyliner is usually the simplest way into the city. It's a limited express with reserved seats only, running between the airport and Nippori / Keisei Ueno. Keisei says it reaches central Tokyo in as little as 36 minutes.

The short version

  • A Skyliner ride is a ¥1,280 fare plus a ¥1,300 liner ticket: ¥2,580 per adult each way (¥2,567 with an IC card fare).
  • Every seat is reserved. Boarding without a liner ticket costs the fee plus a ¥300 surcharge.
  • The ticketless service covers the seat only; you still need a fare ticket or IC card for the gate.
  • Get off at Nippori for JR lines, or ride to Keisei Ueno for Ueno and Asakusa.
  • Keisei says the Skyliner reaches central Tokyo in as little as 36 minutes.

01What the Skyliner costs

The one thing visitors regularly get wrong is the price. A Skyliner ride is two charges added together, and many people only budget for one. Here's the real cost and how buying works, checked on Keisei's official site on October 30, 2026.

Between Keisei Ueno / Nippori and Narita Airport (Terminal 2·3 or Terminal 1), Keisei's official fare table lists:

In words: with a paper ticket the ride comes to ¥2,580 for adults and ¥1,290 for children. If you pay the fare part with an IC card, it comes to ¥2,567 and ¥1,283.

So a couple pays ¥5,160 each way, and a return trip is ¥5,160 per adult. That's the figure our Tokyo itinerary budgets for airport transfers.

The fare is the ordinary train ticket, and you can pay it by tapping an IC card at the gate. The liner ticket is the reserved-seat charge for the Skyliner itself. Every Skyliner seat is reserved, and if you board without a liner ticket Keisei charges the limited-express fee plus a surcharge (¥300 for adults, ¥150 for children). Buy the liner ticket before you board.

Some Skyliners also stop at Aoto and Shin-Kamagaya, which have their own lower fares. Most visitors won't need them.

02Where to buy

Keisei lists several ways to buy:

  • At the station. Liner tickets are sold at the Skyliner stations (Keisei Ueno, Nippori, Airport Terminal 2 and Narita Airport, plus Aoto and Shin-Kamagaya) and Keisei Travel offices, from one month before your travel date.
  • Ticketless service. Register online with a credit card and reserve the liner ticket on your phone, then board with the phone screen. This only covers the seat: you still need a fare ticket (or an IC card) to pass the gate.
  • Skyliner e-ticket "Face Check in Go". Register your face in advance and walk through with face recognition. This one is a one-way fare and liner ticket together. It only works at Keisei Ueno, Nippori, Airport Terminal 2 and Narita Airport stations.
  • Travel agents in Japan sell liner tickets too, and some charge their own fee.

Keisei also sells a set that combines a Skyliner ticket with the Tokyo Subway Ticket at a discount. We didn't confirm the set's 2026 price, so check before you go. For reference, the Tokyo Subway Ticket on its own is ¥1,000 / ¥1,500 / ¥2,000 for 24 / 48 / 72 hours of unlimited Tokyo Metro and Toei subway rides. It doesn't cover JR lines.

03Ueno or Nippori?

The Skyliner's two city stops are Nippori and Keisei Ueno.

  • Get off at Nippori if you're changing to JR. It connects to the JR Yamanote Line, so it's handy for Shinjuku, Shibuya or Tokyo Station.
  • Ride to Keisei Ueno if your hotel is around Ueno or Asakusa, or you're heading to Ueno's museums and Ameyoko.

Exact transfer times depend on your hotel and arrival time. Use a route planner on the day, and check before you go.

04Is it worth it?

Whether ¥2,580 is worth it depends on where you're staying and how much luggage you have. If you're staying on the east side, the Skyliner is straightforward. Every seat is reserved, so you're guaranteed a seat, and you only need to make one decision at the airport. If your hotel is near another line, compare it with other routes for your exact address. We only verified Skyliner prices for this guide, so we haven't compared it with the other options here.

05A first evening that works with it

Our Tokyo plan uses the Skyliner both ways, because the first and last days centre on the east side:

  • Arrival day: Skyliner into Ueno, drop the bags, then Asakusa after dark. Senso-ji, with Tokyo Skytree lit up nearby, is a gentle first evening that doesn't need booking far ahead.
  • Departure day: shopping in Ueno in the morning, then the Skyliner from Keisei Ueno around 13:00.

If it rains on arrival day, the plan switches to Tokyo Solamachi and Sumida Aquarium (¥2,700) under Skytree, all indoors.

06Quick checklist

  1. Budget ¥2,580 per adult each way, not ¥1,300 or ¥1,280.
  2. Buy the liner ticket before boarding, since all seats are reserved.
  3. With the ticketless service, you still need a fare ticket or IC card.
  4. Decide on Nippori or Ueno before you board.
  5. Check Keisei's site for the current Skyliner and subway-ticket set price.
