JR Pass or IC card?

JR
The 2026 fare mathsChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

For the classic first trip of Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka in a week, the 7-day JR Pass costs more than the tickets it replaces. An IC card and two Shinkansen tickets bought separately come out well ahead. Here is the maths, using fares checked today.

The short version

  • A 7-day ordinary JR Pass is ¥50,000 on the official site.
  • Tokyo to Kyoto and Shin-Osaka back to Tokyo on the Nozomi costs ¥28,490 booked on smartEX at regular-season prices.
  • The pass doesn't cover the Nozomi without a ¥4,960 extra ticket per ride on that route.
  • Carry an IC card for everything else: Welcome Suica in Tokyo, ICOCA in Kansai.

01What the pass costs now

The Japan Rail Pass for ordinary cars costs ¥50,000 for 7 days, ¥80,000 for 14 days and ¥100,000 for 21 days when bought on the official JAPAN RAIL PASS website. Exchange orders bought through agents overseas cost more: ¥53,000, ¥84,000 and ¥105,000. The Green Car version is ¥70,000, ¥110,000 and ¥140,000.

Older articles that say the pass always pays for itself were written before these prices. Do the sums again with the figures above.

02The Nozomi catch

The Nozomi is the fastest train on the Tokaido Shinkansen between Tokyo, Kyoto and Shin-Osaka, and the pass does not cover it on its own. To ride it you need a special NOZOMI MIZUHO ticket on top of the pass. It costs ¥4,960 between Tokyo and Kyoto or Shin-Osaka, ¥4,180 between Tokyo and Nagoya and ¥6,500 between Tokyo and Hiroshima. You buy it before boarding, from one month ahead, at JR ticket machines or counters.

The alternative is the Hikari, which the pass does cover. It makes more stops on the same line.

03The maths for a first trip

Take the usual route: Tokyo to Kyoto, a few days in Kyoto and Osaka, then Shin-Osaka back to Tokyo. These are the JR fares you would actually pay, with the Shinkansen booked on smartEX, JR Central's online service, at regular-season prices for an adult reserved seat.

RideFare (adult)
Tokyo → Kyoto, Nozomi, smartEX reserved¥13,970
Kyoto → Osaka, JR local and rapid trains¥580
Tennoji → Nara and back, JR¥1,020
Tennoji → Kansai Airport, JR (if you fly out of KIX)¥1,060
Shin-Osaka → Tokyo, Nozomi, smartEX reserved¥14,520
Total¥31,150

With the pass you would still pay ¥4,960 for each Nozomi ride on this route, or take the Hikari instead.

That is ¥31,150 of JR travel against ¥50,000 for the pass, and the gap is ¥18,850. A Nara day trip from Osaka Station rather than Tennoji, or an extra Kyoto–Osaka run, adds a few hundred yen. It doesn't add eighteen thousand. smartEX prices also move by a few hundred yen depending on whether the date falls in an off-peak, regular or peak season.

The pass wins when the long legs pile up. Add up the smartEX fares for the Shinkansen rides you plan within seven days. If they come close to ¥50,000 before local trains, buy the pass. That usually means going well past Osaka, or making more than one return trip from Tokyo.

04Which IC card in 2026

An IC card is a prepaid card you tap at the ticket gate. It works on trains, subways and buses across Japan's IC areas, and in many convenience stores. You need one whether or not you buy a pass, because the JR Pass doesn't cover the Tokyo subway.

Welcome Suica is JR East's card for short-term visitors. There is no deposit, it is valid for 28 days from the date you buy it, and you load ¥1,000 to ¥10,000 when you buy it. It is sold from machines at Narita Airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2·3 stations and Haneda Airport Terminal 3 on the Tokyo Monorail. It is also sold at JR East Travel Service Centers, including those at Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ikebukuro and Ueno.

Welcome Suica Mobile is the iPhone version. You set it up in the app, it lasts 180 days, there is no deposit, and you top it up from a card in Apple Pay. Unused balance is not refunded, so top it up in small amounts towards the end of the trip.

ICOCA is JR West's card for Osaka and Kyoto. It costs ¥2,000, including a ¥500 deposit that you get back when you return the card. You have to pay in cash, not by credit card. JR West also sells an original-design ICOCA for foreign visitors at the JR ticket office in Kansai Airport Station, one per person, on a non-Japanese passport.

You don't need both. Both cards belong to the nationwide IC network, and JR East lists the ICOCA area among the places Welcome Suica works. Buy whichever is easier where you land.

Since JR East's March 14, 2026 fare change, IC fares are never higher than paper tickets. On a 1–3 km ride you pay ¥155 by card or ¥160 for a ticket.

05Kansai: the old area pass is gone

JR West stopped selling the Kansai Area Pass on September 30, 2026. Its replacement, the WEST QR Kansai Area Pass, costs ¥2,700 for 1 day, ¥4,700 for 2, ¥5,500 for 3 and ¥6,700 for 4. It needs a smartphone, it is sold only through travel agencies overseas, and it doesn't cover the Shinkansen. It includes non-reserved seats on the HARUKA airport express, with two reserved-seat bookings.

Check it against your real rides before you buy. Osaka to Kyoto on JR is ¥580, so an ordinary sightseeing day rarely reaches ¥2,700 on JR alone. The pass pays off on days built around the HARUKA from the airport plus another long JR ride. On a normal Kyoto day, pay as you go. The private Hankyu line, which the pass doesn't cover, runs from Osaka-umeda to Kyoto-kawaramachi for ¥410.

06Tokyo: a subway pass instead

In Tokyo most sightseeing is on the subway rather than JR. The Tokyo Subway Ticket covers all Tokyo Metro and Toei lines for 24, 48 or 72 hours at ¥1,000, ¥1,500 or ¥2,000 for an adult. It is sold to overseas visitors. Use your IC card for the JR rides it doesn't cover.
