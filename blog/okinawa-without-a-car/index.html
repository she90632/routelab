Okinawa without a car

OKA
A monorail and one busChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

AI-assisted content: drafted with AI tools, then fact-checked and edited by Route Lab.

Most Okinawa advice starts with renting a car. You don't need one for a first short trip. Naha has a monorail from the airport to Shuri Castle, and one express bus reaches the Churaumi Aquarium in the north. Choose the right hotel and the right bus, and the car becomes optional.

The short version

  • Stay near a Yui Rail station. Naha Airport to Kencho-mae is ¥290 and 13 minutes.
  • Yui Rail takes Suica and the other national IC cards. Okinawa's buses use their own card, OKICA.
  • For Churaumi, take the 8:40 Yanbaru Express from Kencho-kitaguchi, which arrives at Kinen-koen-mae at 10:45. No booking is needed.

01Stay by the monorail

Yui Rail, Naha's monorail, runs from Naha Airport through the city to Shuri and beyond. Kencho-mae station sits at one end of Kokusai-dori, the main shopping street, which makes it the most useful base. The ride from the airport takes 13 minutes and costs ¥290 for an adult and ¥150 for a child. From Kencho-mae, Makishi, for the public market, is ¥250, and Shuri is ¥320 and 14 minutes.

An adult can bring two children under 6 for free. With a hotel near a station you can arrive, drop your bags and be on Kokusai-dori within the hour.

Budget for a new tax if you travel from February 2027. From February 1, 2027, Okinawa Prefecture charges a lodging tax of 2% of the room-only price per person per night, up to ¥2,000. Where the city or town also charges its own lodging tax, the prefecture's rate is 0.8%, up to ¥800. It covers hotels, ryokan, guesthouses and private rentals, and it applies to nights from February 1 even if you booked before then.

02Which card works where

Yui Rail accepts Suica, ICOCA, PASMO and the other national IC cards, including child Suica cards at the child fare. It does not sell them, so bring yours from the mainland or set up a mobile card before you fly.

Buses are different. Naha Bus's payment page lists OKICA, Okinawa's own IC card, and OKICA's FAQ says it can't be used in place of Suica and the other mainland cards, or the other way round. Have coins and ¥1,000 notes ready for buses. Some routes now also take contactless credit cards at the reader, including route 117 to the aquarium area and route 111.

03Day passes: buy them in the afternoon

Yui Rail's 1-day pass costs ¥1,000, or ¥500 for a child, and the 2-day pass costs ¥1,800 or ¥900. The detail that matters: the 1-day pass lasts 24 hours from purchase, not until midnight.

That changes the maths. One day of Naha sightseeing rarely costs more than ¥1,000 in single fares. Buy the pass when you land in the afternoon, though, and it covers the ride from the airport, an evening trip to Makishi and back, and the next morning's return trip to Shuri. In single fares that is ¥1,430. The pass also gets you discounts, such as ¥160 instead of ¥200 at the Fukushuen garden.

For buses, Naha Bus sells a 1-day pass for its routes within Naha city at ¥840, and a Bus-Mono pass that adds a day of Yui Rail for ¥1,500. Neither covers express buses. On Sundays and public holidays, each passenger paying an adult fare on Naha Bus and Ryukyu Bus can take up to three children of primary-school age or younger free, except on express buses.

04Shuri Castle: the new main hall

Shuri Castle's rebuilt main hall, the Seiden, opens to the public on November 23, 2026. From that date the paid area costs ¥1,000 for adults, ¥760 for high school students and ¥370 for primary and junior high students, and under-6s go free. Older guides quote the earlier ¥400 price.

To go inside the Seiden you need a timed reservation, booked online in 30-minute slots. Slots up to December 31 went on sale in two waves, on September 1 and October 1 (13:00 JST), so book as soon as your dates are fixed. Pre-schoolers don't need a reservation, and the rest of the paid area doesn't need one either.

05Churaumi Aquarium by express bus

The aquarium is about two hours north of Naha, and this is the trip that makes people rent a car. The Yanbaru Express bus does it without one. There are no reservations: you board at the stop and pay for everyone in your group as you get on. Primary and junior high school children pay half, and infants on a lap ride free. The company's fare example from Naha Airport to Kinen-koen-mae, the aquarium stop, is ¥2,000.

The timetable valid from November 1, 2025 has buses leaving Kencho-kitaguchi, near Kencho-mae station, at 6:40, 8:40, 10:20, 11:40 and 12:40. The 8:40 reaches Kinen-koen-mae at 10:45. Coming back, buses leave Kinen-koen-mae at 13:15, 14:15, 15:15, 16:15 and 17:15. They reach Kencho-kitaguchi at 15:30, 16:30, 17:30, 18:32 and 19:30. The buses don't wait for flights, so don't plan the aquarium for the day you fly home.

Churaumi Aquarium costs ¥2,180 for adults, ¥1,440 for high school students and ¥710 for primary and junior high students, and under-6s go free. It is open 8:30–18:30, with last entry at 17:30, and you can leave and come back in on the same day. That lets you walk to the fukugi tree lane at Bise and come back to the tanks.

06Naha on foot

The rest of Naha is walkable from Kencho-mae. Fukushuen, a Chinese-style garden near Naminoue Shrine, costs ¥200 and is open 9:00–21:00, but it is closed on Wednesdays. Tsuboya Yachimun Street, the pottery lane off Kokusai-dori, is fine to walk, but the Tsuboya Pottery Museum is closed for works from September 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027.
