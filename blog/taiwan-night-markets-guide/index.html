Taiwan night markets

The most useful thing to know about night markets in Taiwan is that Tainan and Taipei work differently. Taipei's big markets open every night. Tainan's biggest ones take turns, so the day of the week decides where you eat.

The short version

  • Tainan: Garden Night Market runs Thursday to Sunday, Dadong on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, and Wusheng on Wednesday and Saturday, plus Friday according to the city.
  • Taipei: Raohe opens daily from 17:00 to midnight, and Linjiang Street from 18:00 to midnight.
  • Plan the night market first and build the rest of the day around it, especially in Tainan.

01Tainan's markets take turns

Tainan has no single market that is always open. Its three big ones each set up on certain days of the week. If you are in the city for two nights, those two weekdays decide which markets you can go to.

Friday is the one night when all three may be open, if the city market office's listing for Wusheng holds. Monday and Tuesday leave you with Dadong only, which is no hardship, but plan for it.

02Two nights in Tainan, by arrival day

Most visitors have one or two evenings. Work out which nights you have, then read across the table. A few combinations come up again and again.

Thursday and Friday: Garden on Thursday while it is quieter, then Dadong on Friday for something different. Saturday and Sunday: Garden on one night. On Saturday, Wusheng is the alternative if you would rather skip the weekend crowd. Sunday and Monday: Garden, then Dadong. Tuesday and Wednesday: Dadong, then Wusheng, which is the only pairing that leaves Garden out entirely.

If you only have one evening and it falls on a Thursday to Sunday, go to Garden. It is the one with the widest choice, and it gives you a reason to come back.

03Garden Night Market (Huayuan)

Garden is the biggest and the one most visitors mean when they talk about Tainan night markets. Tainan's tourism bureau lists it as open Thursday to Sunday, 17:00 to midnight. Friday was added on October 3, 2025. Some older pages, including parts of the city's own tourism site, still say Thursday, Saturday and Sunday, so don't be thrown when you see that.

It is large and busy at the weekend. Go on Thursday or Friday if you want to walk at your own pace, and go early in the evening if you are with children.

04Dadong Night Market

Dadong sets up on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, which makes it the start-of-week market. Opening times differ between sources: the tourism bureau and other listings disagree by an hour. We have left them out. Arrive after dark and you won't be early.

05Wusheng Night Market

Wusheng started in 1984 and has about 330 stalls, according to the Tainan City Market Office. The office lists it as open on Wednesday, Friday and Saturday. Some tourism pages still say Wednesday and Saturday only. If Friday matters to your plan, choose Garden or Dadong that night and treat Wusheng as a bonus.

It is the natural Wednesday choice, since neither of the others opens that night.

06Taipei: every night, so pick by area

In Taipei the question isn't which day but which part of the city you will be in at dinner time.

Raohe Street Night Market, in Songshan, is open daily from 17:00 to midnight, according to the Taipei city tourism site. The same site singles out pepper buns baked in a clay oven, herbal pork-rib soup, oyster vermicelli and stinky tofu. Walk the whole length before you decide what to eat, then work your way back.

Linjiang Street Night Market, also called Tonghua, is in Da'an between Tonghua Street and Keelung Road. It is open daily from 18:00 to midnight. It is the easy choice if you have been around Taipei 101 or the east side during the day.

Shilin and Ningxia are the other two names you will hear. Both are well worth a visit, but the official and tourism sources we checked give different hours, so we have left them out rather than guess. Check on the day.

07When to arrive

Opening time is when the stalls start setting up, not when the market is at its best. In the first hour you get space to look and short queues. Later you get the crowd and the noise that people come for. With children or older travellers, go at opening and leave as it fills up. Otherwise, eat a light late lunch and arrive an hour or so after opening.

08How to eat at a night market

Share everything. Portions are sized for walking and eating, and the point is to try six things rather than finish two. Carry cash in small notes and don't count on paying by card at the stalls. Bring a small bag for your rubbish, because bins can be hard to find in the crowd.

The markets are outdoors, so heavy rain thins out both stalls and crowds. If the weather turns, eat at a restaurant nearby and come back the next night it opens. In Tainan, check the table above first.
