Kamakura day trip from Tokyo

KMK
One JR line, no bookingChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

AI-assisted content: drafted with AI tools, then fact-checked and edited by Route Lab.

Kamakura is the classic day out from Tokyo. It has a giant bronze Buddha sitting in the open air, a big shrine at the top of a long avenue, and a small seaside train that runs along the coast to Enoshima. It's one JR line from Tokyo Station, and you don't need a tour, a pass or a reservation for any of it.

The short version

  • JR from Tokyo Station to Kamakura is ¥1,040 each way since the March 14, 2026 fare change.
  • The Great Buddha costs ¥300 (Oct–Mar 8:00–17:00), plus ¥50 to go inside. No food on the grounds.
  • Tsurugaoka Hachimangu's gates are open 06:00–20:00 all year, 10 minutes' walk from the station.
  • The Enoden Noriorikun day pass is ¥800. A full day costs about ¥3,180 per adult before food.

01Getting there: ¥1,040 each way

What you do need is up-to-date prices. JR East changed its fares on March 14, 2026, so older guides understate the train cost. Here's the 2026 version, checked on official sites on October 30, 2026.

The simplest route is the JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station to Kamakura. It's about 51 km, which falls in the 51–60 km fare band. Under JR East's March 2026 fare revision that band costs ¥1,040 each way, or ¥2,080 return. You can tap in with an IC card.

Start early. Our plan has you at Kamakura Station around 08:30, so the shrine and the Buddha both fit comfortably before lunch.

02Tsurugaoka Hachimangu

Kamakura's main shrine is about 10 minutes on foot from the east exit of JR Kamakura Station, according to the shrine. The gates are open 06:00–20:00 all year, so it's an easy first stop, even very early.

The official site lists fees only for the facilities inside the grounds:

  • Treasure Hall (Homotsuden): ¥200 adults, ¥100 elementary school children, 9:00–16:00. It's normally open every day but closes when exhibitions change.
  • Kamakura Bunkakan Tsurugaoka Museum: 10:00–16:30, last entry 16:00. Closed Mondays (open on public holidays), between exhibitions and over New Year. The price depends on the exhibition. It takes about 30–40 minutes.

03The Great Buddha (Kotoku-in)

The Great Buddha of Kamakura is a National Treasure. It's a bronze Amida Buddha whose casting began in 1252, and the hall around it was brought down by a typhoon and a tsunami, which is why it now sits outdoors. The temple's official site lists:

  • Entry: adults ¥300, elementary school children ¥150, preschoolers free. Going inside the statue costs an extra ¥50.
  • Hours: Oct–Mar 8:00–17:00, Apr–Sep 8:00–17:30. Last entry is 15 minutes before closing.
  • Inside the Buddha: 8:00–16:30 all year.
  • No booking needed.

Eating and drinking aren't allowed on the grounds, apart from drinks of water. Have lunch before or after your visit.

Getting there: take the Enoden line from Kamakura to Hase Station, then walk about 7 minutes. Or take a bus from the Kamakura Station east exit, Enoden bus from stop 1 or Keikyu bus from stop 6, to the "Daibutsu-mae" stop. Hase-dera temple is about 5 minutes' walk from the Buddha if you want another stop nearby. Check Hase-dera's own fees and hours before you go.

04The Enoden day pass: ¥800

The Enoden is the little train that runs between Kamakura and Fujisawa, partly along the sea. Its one-day pass, Noriorikun, costs ¥800 for adults and ¥400 for children. It gives unlimited rides on the whole line for one day, and you can get off at any station.

  • Buy it from the ticket machines at any Enoden station, or as a digital ticket on a smartphone through the EMot app or website.
  • It's only valid on the day of issue, and there are no refunds once you've started travelling.
  • The pass also comes with discounts at over 50 places along the line.

Does it pay off? It depends how many times you get on and off. Kamakura to Hase, Hase to Kamakurakokomae, then on to Enoshima adds up to three rides, and the pass also lets you change your mind on the way. Compare the single fares on Enoden's site if you only plan one or two rides.

Our favourite stop is Kamakurakokomae, for the ocean view.

05A route that works

Here's one easy order for a full day:

  1. 08:30 Arrive at JR Kamakura (¥1,040 from Tokyo).
  2. Morning: walk to Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, about 10 minutes.
  3. Back at Kamakura Station: buy the Noriorikun pass (¥800) and ride the Enoden to Hase.
  4. Around midday: the Great Buddha (¥300). Eat before or after, since food isn't allowed on the grounds.
  5. Afternoon: Enoden to Kamakurakokomae for the sea view, then on to Enoshima.
  6. Evening: back to Tokyo by JR (¥1,040).

Day cost per adult: about ¥3,180 for the JR return, the Enoden pass and the Great Buddha. Add ¥50 if you go inside the statue and ¥200 for the shrine's Treasure Hall. Food isn't included.

Train times and connections vary, so use a route planner on the day: check before you go.

06Rain or crowds?

The Great Buddha is outdoors and the Enoden coast is all about the view, so Kamakura is a fine-weather day. If rain is forecast, move it to another day if you can. Our Tokyo itinerary's rain plan for this day swaps the trip out for Ueno's museums and Ameyoko.
