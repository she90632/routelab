Osaka and Kyoto in 4 days

OSA
One hotel, two day tripsChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 5 min read · Route Lab

The most common first-timer mistake in Kansai is splitting four days between a Kyoto hotel and an Osaka hotel. Kyoto is a short train ride from Osaka, and so is Nara. Stay in one place, pack once, and spend the time you save on the places themselves.

The short version

  • Base yourself near Osaka Station in Umeda. JR to Kyoto is ¥580 and Hankyu is ¥410.
  • Do Kyoto on your first full day and start early: Fushimi Inari never closes and Kiyomizu-dera opens at 6:00.
  • Todai-ji in Nara takes cash only, and from November to March it closes at 17:00.

01Why one hotel in Umeda

Umeda is the area around JR Osaka Station and the Hankyu and Hanshin terminals. From there the JR train to Kyoto costs ¥580, and the Hankyu line from Osaka-umeda to Kyoto-kawaramachi costs ¥410. Kawaramachi drops you in central Kyoto, a short walk from Nishiki Market and Gion, which makes it the easier way home in the evening.

Nara is just as reachable. JR from Tennoji to Nara is ¥510, and Tennoji is a few stops round the Osaka Loop Line. The airport is covered too: Tennoji to Kansai Airport is ¥1,060 on JR.

Moving hotels costs half a day: check-out, a train with suitcases, and waiting for a check-in time. With one base you lose none of that, and the day trips can swap around when the weather changes.

02Pay with ICOCA, not a pass

Buy an ICOCA, JR West's IC card, when you land. It costs ¥2,000, which includes a ¥500 deposit, and it works on JR, Hankyu, the Osaka Metro and Kyoto's buses. You tap in and out, and you never have to work out a fare at a ticket machine.

Don't plan around the JR Kansai Area Pass. JR West stopped selling that pass on September 30, 2026, so older guides that build a trip on it are out of date. For four days in Kansai, single fares on an IC card are simpler. Our JR Pass vs IC card guide has the fare maths if you are also visiting Tokyo.

03Day 1: arrive, then go out in Osaka

Most flights land in the afternoon, so the first day is short. Drop your bags and go to Dotonbori, the canal street with the Glico running man sign. It costs nothing to walk around, and it is at its best after dark.

If you would rather see the city from above, Umeda Sky Building's Kuchu Teien observatory is in Umeda itself. It costs ¥2,000 for an adult and ¥500 for children from age 4 through primary school. Under-4s go free. It is open 9:30–22:30, with last entry at 22:00, so it fits a late arrival. You enter through the observatory's own entrance and buy tickets on the 39th floor.

04Day 2: Kyoto, and start early

Kyoto rewards an early start more than any other city in Japan. Fushimi Inari Taisha, the shrine with thousands of orange gates, is free and its grounds are open at all hours, with no closing days. JR Inari Station on the Nara Line is at the foot of the shrine. Go at 8:00 or 9:00 and the lower gates are far quieter than at noon.

From there, head to Kiyomizu-dera. It opens at 6:00 and costs ¥500 for adults and ¥200 for primary and junior high school students. From November 21 to 30 the temple also opens at night for the autumn season, until 21:30.

If you take a bus inside Kyoto, the city bus charges a flat ¥230 for adults and ¥120 for children. The Subway and Bus One-Day Pass costs ¥1,100 for adults and ¥550 for children. It covers the calendar day, not 24 hours. At ¥230 a ride, the pass only pays off from your fifth bus ride. Most first-timers taking trains to the big sights won't get there.

Prefer bamboo and gardens? Go to Arashiyama instead. The Tenryu-ji garden costs ¥500 from high school age and ¥300 for primary and junior high students. It is open 8:30–17:00, with reception closing at 16:50. Either way, take Hankyu back to Umeda in the evening.

05Day 3: Nara's deer, or Osaka Castle

Nara is the easiest day trip from Osaka. The deer in Nara Park bow for crackers, which cost ¥200 a bundle. Todai-ji's Great Buddha Hall costs ¥800 for adults and ¥400 for children aged 6 to 12. From November to March it is open 8:00–17:00, and from April to October 7:30–17:30. Todai-ji's Japanese admissions page says it takes cash only, so carry yen.

Staying in Osaka is the other choice. The Osaka Castle Main Tower is ¥1,200 for an adult, and children aged 15 and under get in free with ID. It is open 9:00–18:00, with last entry at 17:30, and closed from December 28 to January 1. Shinsekai and Tsutenkaku are a short JR ride away, which makes a full day in the south of the city.

06If it rains

Rain changes the Kyoto day the most, because Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama are both outdoors. Sanjusangendo is the indoor swap. It costs ¥600 for adults, ¥400 for junior high and high school students, and ¥300 for primary school pupils. From November 16 to March 31 it is open 9:00–16:00, with reception closing at 15:30. After that, Nishiki Market is a covered street a short train ride away.

In Osaka, Harukas 300 at Abeno Harukas is the rain plan with a view, and it is indoors from the station up. Since July 23, 2026 an adult ticket costs ¥2,200 at the counter or ¥1,980 bought online in advance. It is not worth it in low cloud, so check the sky before you go.

07Day 4: Kuromon Market, then the airport

Spend the last morning at Kuromon Market in Namba, a covered market of fish, fruit and snack stalls. Each shop keeps its own hours, so most of the market is open by mid-morning. If your bags are at the hotel, the Midosuji Line runs straight from Namba back to Umeda.

Give the airport trip a margin. Kansai Airport is on an island in the bay, well south of the city, so leave Umeda earlier than feels necessary.
