Tokyo on a rainy day

TYO
Indoor plans that workChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

Rain in Tokyo rarely ruins a day. It just punishes plans that need you to walk between ten places. Pick one building or one station, stay there for half a day, and the weather stops mattering.

The short version

  • Check closing days first: the Tokyo National Museum is shut on Mondays.
  • teamLab Planets prices change by date and sell out, so buy before the forecast firms up.
  • No booking at all? Go to Tokyo Station. Ramen, sweets and character shops sit together outside the ticket gates.

01When the rain is likely

Tokyo gets rain in every month, but the long wet stretch is tsuyu, the early-summer rainy season. The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991–2020 average puts the start for the Kanto-Koshin region, which includes Tokyo, around June 7 and the end around July 19. In 2026 the agency's dates were around June 2 to around July 19.

Those dates describe a season, not three weeks of downpour. Tsuyu tends to mean low cloud, drizzle and the odd dry afternoon. That is why the plans below are built around clusters: one place where you can spend three or four hours without going back outside, plus a short, covered way to reach it.

02Ueno: one museum, a whole morning

The Tokyo National Museum in Ueno Park is the most dependable rainy-day plan in the city. The collection exhibitions cost ¥1,000 for an adult, and the museum is big enough that a morning goes quickly. It opens at 9:30 and closes at 17:00, but stays open until 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays, so it also works as an evening plan when the weather turns in the afternoon.

Watch the calendar. The museum is closed on Mondays, or on the Tuesday when Monday is a public holiday. In 2026 it also closes on December 15 and for the year-end from December 26 to 31. It does not allow suitcases inside, so don't try to fit it in on the way to or from the airport.

The museum says it is a 10-minute walk from JR Ueno or Uguisudani Station. That is the only stretch in the open, so one umbrella covers the whole morning.

03Skytree: an aquarium inside a mall

Sumida Aquarium is on the 5th and 6th floors of Tokyo Solamachi, the shopping complex at the base of Tokyo Skytree. An adult ticket is ¥2,700. The price went up from ¥2,500 on February 10, 2026, so older guides quote the wrong figure.

Being inside a mall is the point. Lunch, the aquarium and an hour of shopping happen under one roof. If the cloud is low, skip the Skytree observation decks that day, because the view on a grey day is mostly the inside of the cloud. Keep them for a clear morning later in the trip.

04teamLab Planets: decide early

teamLab Planets in Toyosu is the obvious rainy-day booking, and that is its problem: everyone has the same idea. Adult tickets start at ¥3,800, and the price changes by date, so a wet weekend can cost more than a dry weekday. Buy online in advance rather than turning up.

teamLab describes it as a museum where you walk through water, so wear trousers you can roll up. If one day of your trip looks wet and you want to go, book that day as soon as you can and move the outdoor plans to the other days. Waiting for a certain forecast usually means waiting until the good slots are gone.

05Tokyo Station: the plan that needs no booking

If you only remember one rainy-day fallback, make it First Avenue Tokyo Station. It is the shopping and food area inside JR Tokyo Station, on the Yaesu side, outside the ticket gates, so you can wander around without paying a fare.

Three parts are worth the trip. Tokyo Ramen Street is open 10:30–23:00 with last orders at 22:30. It doesn't take QR-code payments, so have a card or cash ready. Tokyo Character Street, with shops for anime and TV characters, is open 10:00–20:30. Tokyo Okashi Land, for packaged sweets and snacks that make easy gifts, is open 9:00–21:00. Individual shops can keep shorter hours, so treat these as the outer limits.

Tokyo Station also works for the last day of a trip, when you have bags, an afternoon train and a forecast you can't do anything about.

06Shibuya without the crossing

Shibuya on a wet evening is mostly umbrellas, but one building makes it worth it. Shibuya PARCO has Nintendo TOKYO and Pokémon Center SHIBUYA on the same floor, the 6th. If anyone in your group cares about either, that floor is an hour of their trip sorted, and the rest of PARCO is right there.

07Getting around when it's wet

The cheapest way to walk less is to ride more. The Tokyo Subway Ticket covers every Tokyo Metro and Toei subway line for 24, 48 or 72 hours. It costs ¥1,000, ¥1,500 or ¥2,000 for an adult. It is sold to overseas visitors, and JR lines such as the Yamanote Line are not included.

JR fares in the Tokyo area went up on March 14, 2026. A ride of 1–3 km on JR East now costs ¥155 on an IC card and ¥160 with a paper ticket. If the rain has you hopping one or two stations at a time, an IC card is still the simplest way to pay. Our JR Pass vs IC card guide covers which card to get.
