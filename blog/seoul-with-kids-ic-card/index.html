Seoul with kids

SEL
Cards, child fares, free stopsChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

AI-assisted content: drafted with AI tools, then fact-checked and edited by Route Lab.

Seoul is an easy city to do with children on public transport. The subway is clean and frequent, and children ride for a fraction of the adult fare. The catch is that the discount isn't automatic: a child's transport card has to be registered first, or it charges the adult price.

The short version

  • Under-6s ride the Seoul subway free, up to three per adult.
  • Children aged 6 to 12 pay ₩550 by card, and 13 to 18-year-olds pay ₩900, but only once the card has their date of birth registered.
  • The National Museum of Korea's Children's Museum is free but booked online, and slots open 14 days ahead at midnight.

02Register the card, or pay adult fares

The card most visitors use is T-money. It is sold at convenience stores such as GS25, CU and 7-Eleven, and at subway stations, and costs ₩3,000–5,000 depending on the design. You then add credit in cash.

Korea's tourism organisation says that travellers under 18 must register their date of birth at a convenience store to get the discount. An unregistered card charges the adult fare, so a child on an unregistered card pays ₩1,550 a ride instead of ₩550. Register each child's card when you buy it, before the first ride, and keep the cards apart so nobody taps the wrong one.

A single-journey ticket is not a way round this. For teenagers it costs ₩1,650, the same as an adult's single ticket. Over a typical day of four rides, a child on a registered card pays ₩2,200 and a teenager ₩3,600. On unregistered cards each would pay ₩6,200.

03Under-6s ride free

Seoul Metro lets children under 6 ride free, up to three per adult guardian. A fourth pre-schooler with the same adult pays the child fare. Two adults with three small children pay ₩3,100 per subway ride for the whole group. Go through the wide gate together, and keep a passport or other proof of age handy in case staff ask.

04Transfers are part of the fare

Seoul's fares are distance-based with free transfers. If you change between subway and bus within 30 minutes, the fare carries on rather than starting again. From 21:00 to 07:00 the window stretches to 60 minutes.

This matters with children because family trips are slow. A toilet stop and a snack between the subway and the bus can push you past 30 minutes, and then you pay twice.

05Is the Climate Card worth it for a family?

Seoul's Climate Card has short-term passes for visitors: ₩5,000 for 1 day, ₩8,000 for 2 days, ₩10,000 for 3 days, ₩15,000 for 5 days and ₩20,000 for 7 days. The physical card costs ₩3,000 on top. It covers the subway and buses in Seoul, though not the Sinbundang Line, the GTX or airport buses. You can recharge it only with cash or a Korean card, and the pass starts on the day you charge it.

For an adult, a 1-day pass at ₩5,000 pays off on the fourth ₩1,550 ride, and the 3-day pass at ₩10,000 pays off on the seventh ride, which is two or three days of ordinary sightseeing. For a child, it doesn't work out. The city's page lists no child price, and at ₩550 a ride a child would need ten rides in a day to beat ₩5,000. The sensible family setup is usually a Climate Card for each adult who rides a lot and a registered T-money card for each child.

06From the airport: AREX with children

The AREX Express train runs non-stop between Seoul Station and Incheon Airport, 43 minutes to Terminal 1. An adult ticket is ₩13,000, and a child aged 6 to under 13 pays ₩9,500. Being non-stop, it is the calmer way in with luggage and tired children.

07Free and cheap stops children like

The National Museum of Korea's permanent galleries are free. It is open 9:30–17:30, and on Wednesdays and Saturdays until 21:00. Its Children's Museum, mainly for ages 5 to 9, is also free but needs an online reservation. There are five 80-minute sessions a day between 9:30 and 17:20. Bookings open at midnight 14 days ahead, for up to five people, and there are no tickets at the door. Adults without children are not admitted. The Children's Museum is closed on December 7, 2026.

Gyeongbokgung Palace is cheap for families. It costs ₩3,000 for ages 19 to 64, ₩1,500 for ages 7 to 18, and nothing for children aged 6 and under, so two adults with children aged 7 and 10 pay ₩9,000 in total. It is closed on Tuesdays, and in November and December it is open 9:00–17:00, with last entry at 16:00.

For a day outdoors, Seoul Children's Grand Park has been free since 2006 except for its paid facilities. The park opens 5:00–22:00 and the zoo 10:00–17:00, and strollers can be hired for ₩3,000. Take Line 7 to Children's Grand Park Station, exit 1.
