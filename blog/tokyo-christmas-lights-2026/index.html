Tokyo Christmas lights 2026

XMS
Two evenings of free lightsChecked 2026-10-30

Facts checked 2026-10-30 · 4 min read · Route Lab

AI-assisted content: drafted with AI tools, then fact-checked and edited by Route Lab.

From November to Christmas, Tokyo puts on some of the best winter illuminations in the world, and most of them are free. The catch is that they're spread across the city and each one keeps its own dates and hours. Some end on December 25, others run into February, and one light show stops before Christmas.

The short version

  • Marunouchi, Hibiya and Ebisu lights run into February; Roppongi Hills and Midtown end on Dec 25.
  • Midtown's light show with bubbles stops on Dec 20, so plan an earlier evening for it.
  • The Shiba Park Christmas market (Dec 4–25) is the one that charges entry: ¥1,000 on weekdays.
  • Start the Roppongi evening at Ebisu, where the lights come on at 16:00, then walk between Keyakizaka and Tokyo Midtown.
  • For two evenings of light-hopping, the 48-hour Tokyo Subway Ticket (¥1,500) is usually the one to buy.

01Marunouchi and Hibiya (near Tokyo Station)

This guide lists the 2026 dates and hours from each venue's official site and groups them into two easy evenings: Marunouchi and Hibiya near Tokyo Station, and Roppongi with Ebisu. Everything here was checked on October 30, 2026. Venues can change hours on crowded nights, so confirm on the day.

If you only have one evening, spend it here. It's all walkable from Tokyo Station.

  • Marunouchi Illumination (Naka-dori): about 1.2 km of street trees in champagne-gold lights. Free. Nov 12, 2026 – Feb 14, 2027, 16:00–23:00, and until 24:00 through December. Late in the evening it's noticeably quieter.
  • Christmas market and tree on Gyoko-dori: the Marunouchi Street Park Christmas market, on the wide avenue between Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace. Nov 27 – Dec 25, 11:00–22:00. Free to walk in; food and drinks are paid.
  • HIBIYA Magic Time Illumination (Tokyo Midtown Hibiya): free. Nov 12, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027, 16:00–23:00, at Hibiya Step Plaza, the 6th-floor Park View Garden and Hibiya Naka-dori. The big tree in the plaza stays up until Dec 25.

A good order: Hibiya around dusk, then walk north along Naka-dori under the Marunouchi lights, and finish at the Gyoko-dori market with Tokyo Station's red-brick front lit up behind you.

02Roppongi and Ebisu

Make this your second evening.

  • Keyakizaka Illumination (Roppongi Hills): about 400 m of "SNOW & BLUE" lights with Tokyo Tower at the end of the slope. Free. Nov 5 – Dec 25, 2026, 17:00–22:00. Hours may change without notice when it's crowded.
  • MIDTOWN CHRISTMAS: Midwinter Lights (Tokyo Midtown): free. Nov 26 – Dec 25, 2026, 17:00–23:00. The 4-minute light show with bubbles runs only until Dec 20, so if you want to see it, plan an earlier date.
  • Ebisu Garden Place: free. Lights 16:00–23:00, Nov 14, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027. The 10 m Christmas tree is up until Dec 25. A Christmas marché runs on weekdays, 17:00–20:00, and may close in rain or strong wind.

Keyakizaka and Midtown are a short walk apart, so do them together. Ebisu is a quick ride away and works well as the first stop of the evening, since its lights come on at 16:00.

For a view from above first, Tokyo City View, the Roppongi Hills observation deck, costs ¥2,400 at the counter (¥2,200 online, with timed tickets). It's open 10:00–22:00 with last entry at 21:30. Go up just before sunset, then walk down to Keyakizaka.

03The Christmas market in Shiba Park

Tokyo Christmas Market in Shiba Park runs Dec 4–25, 2026, 11:00–22:00 (last entry 21:00). Unlike the light-ups, it charges entry. Adults pay ¥1,000 on weekdays, ¥1,500 on weekends and ¥2,000 on Dec 24–25. It's one minute from Onarimon station exit A6 on the Toei Mita line. Go on a weekday if you can, for the lower price and smaller crowds.

04Getting around

The Tokyo Subway Ticket gives unlimited rides on all Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines for 24, 48 or 72 hours (¥1,000 / ¥1,500 / ¥2,000 per adult). It doesn't cover JR trains. It's sold to overseas visitors and to some visitors from outside Tokyo, so check the eligibility rules on Tokyo Metro's site. For two evenings of light-hopping, the 48-hour ticket is usually the one to get.

Exact routes and transfers between stops change with your hotel and start time. Use a route planner on the day: check before you go.

05Daytime ideas and weather

The illuminations start around dusk, so plan the daytime around them:

  • Imperial Palace East Gardens: free, no booking. December hours 9:00–16:00 (last entry 15:30). Closed Mondays and Fridays and on some event days.
  • Hama-rikyu Gardens: ¥300, 9:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30).
  • Meiji Jingu: free. The gates follow sunrise and sunset; in December they're open 6:40–16:00.

The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991–2020 averages for Tokyo in December are a daily high of 12.0 °C and a low of 3.8 °C. You'll be outside after dark for hours, so a warm coat, a scarf and comfortable shoes matter more than you'd think.

06What we left out

Some well-known displays from past years, such as Ao no Dokutsu, Caretta Shiodome and the Baccarat chandelier, had no official 2026 information when we checked. They aren't listed here. If they're announced, check before you go.
